## Finding 03 — The bucket that hides it
`FINDING_03_the_bucket_that_hides_it.md` — **CONFIRMED [P].** The NSDUH national
report reports every substance measure at 12-17 / 18-25 / **26 or older**. SAMHSA's
own Table 5.3B disaggregates it: **21-25 at 28.8% and 26-29 at 27.9%** are the
worst-affected bands in the country and appear nowhere in the report, while 65+ sits
at 8.0%. Drug use disorder rose in **every** adult band 26-49 while alcohol fell, so
the headline decline is an alcohol result. Treatment access inside the same bucket
falls threefold from 26-49 (4.7%) to 65+ (1.6%). **Corrects an Aegis error of the
same day.** Opened on Puckett's observation that "26+ is an age range that obfuscates
the truth, it's huge."

## Finding 04 — The door that never reopened
`FINDING_04_the_door_that_never_reopened.md` — **contraction CONFIRMED [P],
overreaction [U].** NY Fed data: the 10th-percentile credit score at mortgage
origination rose ~70 points after 2008 and has not come back in fifteen years;
lending below 660 fell from **24.0% of dollars in 2006 to 4.8% in 2021** and has
never exceeded 10.2% since 2010. Urban's HCAI puts government-channel risk tolerance
at 9.8% against a **pre-bubble** norm of 19-23%. **Against that, realized default on
the tight book is only modestly better than the normal book, the measure is a stock
not a vintage, and it is endogenous** — so "overreaction" is not established and is
barred from OWG output until the vintage series is pulled. Tests Puckett's causal
claim on H-11 and reports the half that cuts against him.

## Finding 05 — The stock and the flow
`FINDING_05_the_stock_and_the_flow.md` — **CONFIRMED [P] with a control.** NSDUH
measures **initiation of use** in detail (Tables A.21-A.23, Figures 31-34, 68 hits on
`initiat*`) and has **zero** measures of **onset of disorder** — it counts the stock,
not the flow, so it cannot say who developed a disorder or after what. And of the
**40.7 million** untreated adults with a past-year SUD, **95.6% (38.1 million) did
not perceive that they needed treatment**; only 0.7% sought it and failed to get it.
**Access is a real barrier and is not the main one at this stage.** Reframes every
remediation proposal in the case file: a treatment appropriation reaches 1.8 million
of 40.7 million. Opened on Puckett's objection to age-banded analysis, which also
produced a standing METHOD rule and forced the withdrawal of Finding 04 sec 9d.

## Finding 06 — The sentence after the sentence
`FINDING_06_the_sentence_after_the_sentence.md` — **CONFIRMED [P] from the
maintaining body's own text.** The National Inventory of Collateral Consequences, run
by CSG Justice Center under BJA funding, states that consequences "frequently apply
**without consideration of the time passed** between the conviction and the
opportunity being sought **or the person's rehabilitation efforts** since the
conviction," and that some apply "without regard to the relationship between the
crime and opportunity being restricted." **15,000+ licensing provisions, 6,000+ of
them mandatory, across a quarter of the labor market** (corroborated independently by
CSG's "nearly 1 in 4 jobs"). Ties to Finding 02: the mechanism the recovery evidence
supports is self-support, and this is the largest legal obstruction to it. Opened on
Puckett's statement that a young adult who changes his life should not pay for the
rest of it.

## Finding 07 — Who is trying
`FINDING_07_who_is_trying.md` — **CONFIRMED [P], controls run.** NIH RePORTER FY2024-25:
pharmacology and medication research draws **$3,172M raw / $1,205M title-confirmed**
against **$459M / $69M** for recovery housing, employment, peer support and recovery
capital combined — **between 6.9 and 17.4 to one.** Recovery housing, the intervention
with a randomized **+$29,000/person** cost-benefit result, gets **$3.8M** across two
fiscal years; `oxford house` returns **nine projects**. Who is trying: two dozen
academic groups on $1-6M grants, and **the VA**, which runs 7 of the 17
employment-specific projects and **reports every one at $0** — a RePORTER artifact
(253 VA projects, none with an award amount) that makes the largest employment-based
recovery program in the federal government invisible to any dollar-weighted analysis.
**Excludes SAMHSA block grants and the ~$50B opioid settlement, which is the next pull.**
Also records a discarded 100%-noise result that would have flattered the thesis.
