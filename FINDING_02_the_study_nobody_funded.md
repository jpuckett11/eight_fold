# EIGHTFOLD / FINDING 02
## Four independent lines of evidence say self-support, not income, is the active ingredient in recovery. The study that would prove it has never been run.

**Status:** The underlying results are **CONFIRMED [P]** from randomized trials, a
cost-benefit analysis and a systematic review. **The model that unifies them is
[I], and this finding does not claim otherwise.**
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-31.
**The model is Jay Puckett's. It is recorded as his and tested against the
literature rather than assumed.**

Registered as H-08, H-09 and H-10 in `HYPOTHESES.md`.
Exhibits in `exhibits/employment/` and `exhibits/barriers/`.

---

## 1. The claim

Stated by Puckett across 2026-08-31, in three parts:

> *"A cert is not a job that is paying right away, or even paying enough for them to
> live comfortably."*

> *"When they can pay their own way and contribute they become a meaningful part of
> society, which in turn gives them the drive and the want to continue not to
> relapse, because then they don't have to worry about imposter syndrome — they are
> doing something."*

> *"When you have the money to be self sufficient and live comfortably you are doing
> things, making friends, colleagues, and you have something."*

**The model.** Income does not reinforce recovery directly. It buys the ability to
participate; participation builds a social network; the network sustains recovery.
And the operative distinction is not how much money arrives but **whether the person
earned it** — because being a contributor rather than a recipient is what changes
who they are to themselves.

    income -> participation and activity -> social network -> recovery
    and: self-supported income != granted income of the same size

## 2. Line one: the certificate does nothing [P]

*International Journal of Offender Therapy and Comparative Criminology* 2023,
DOI 10.1177/0306624X231159886. Prison vocational education, propensity score matched:

> "**After matching, there were no differences in any outcome** between those who
> obtained vocational certificates and the comparison group."

> "The results demonstrate the importance of **accounting for selection bias**."

**The apparent benefit of vocational training is largely the difference between
people who sign up and people who do not.**

## 3. Line two: the two residential models, opposite results [P]

Both randomized. Both in the same domain. **Opposite reward structures, opposite
verdicts.**

| | **Job Corps** | **Oxford House** |
|---|---|---|
| Model | residential, **stipend received**, professional staff | self-governed, **residents pay their own rent**, no staff |
| Design | random assignment, n = 15,386 | random assignment, n = 150 |
| Outcome | 12% year-4 earnings gain, ~$10-11k earnings level | **15.6%** substance abuse at 24 months (6+ mo) vs **64.8%** usual aftercare |
| Durability | **68.3% decay** in administrative earnings impact by year 7 | RCT: OH condition "significantly more likely to remain continuously abstinent" |
| Economics | **"benefits to society... smaller than the substantial program costs"** | **net benefit ~$29,000 per person, statistically significant** |

Sources: National Job Corps Study (ERIC ED498081); *J Subst Abuse Treat* 2013
PMID 22498012; *Eval Program Plann* 2012 PMID 22054524.

**[I] Job Corps supplied housing, training and money and the effect faded. Oxford
House supplies housing and requires the resident to fund it, and the effect held
and paid for itself.**

**Read the length-of-stay figures correctly.** The 15.6%/64.8% contrast compares
people who *stayed* six months against those who did not, and **staying was not
randomized**. The intent-to-treat comparison is separate, weaker in effect size, and
also favors Oxford House. **Both must travel together; the first is not the
randomized result.**

## 4. Line three: self-run beat staff-run, which is the specific prediction [P]

*Frontiers in Public Health* 2025 systematic review, PMID 40115346:

> "Recovery housing interventions performed better than continuing care as usual/no
> intervention on abstinence, **income, employment**, criminal charges... Recovery
> housing also **performed better than comparative interventions delivered in other
> types of residential settings (e.g., therapeutic communities)**."

**Therapeutic communities are professionally staffed. Oxford Houses are not.** A
generic "residential support helps" hypothesis predicts both work. **This model
predicts the self-run one wins, and the review reports that it does.**

## 5. Line four: the pathway is documented, in a randomized sample [P]

*Alcohol Treatment Quarterly* 2016, PMID 28484304. **270 justice-involved
individuals with substance use disorders, randomized** to self-run Oxford Houses, a
staffed therapeutic community, or usual aftercare. Important Persons and Activities
instrument, five waves across two years:

> "Reading/Writing activities, and Exercise/Sports activities, were most reported at
> baseline. **By Wave 5, Education/Work and Interacting with Others were the most
> reported activities.**"

**Baseline activity is solitary. Two years later the top two are work and other
people.** That is the sequence the model predicts, measured with a validated
instrument.

And from the people themselves — *Work* 2017, PMID 28582944, women in sober-living
homes across 20 urban areas:

> "Employment is important to women in substance abuse recovery, **not only as a
> means for financial support, but also as a life priority.**"

> "**employer scheduling practices, low-level positions, and lack of employment
> supports impact recovery.**"

**"Low-level positions" is the wage-adequacy claim, stated by the people it is
about, as something that impedes recovery.**

## 6. The result that appears to contradict all of this, and does not

*Substance Abuse* 2016, PMID 26308507, survival analysis, N = 268:

> "**demographic and employment variables did not significantly predict relapse
> risk**"

**[I] Under a mediation model this is the expected result, not a refutation.** A
variable acting entirely through a mediator shows no direct effect once the mediator
is present. Employment predicts recovery **through** participation and network, so
it should not predict it directly. **The null is evidence for the pathway.**

**That reasoning is also the most dangerous move in this finding**, because it
converts a disconfirmation into a confirmation. It is only legitimate if the
mediation is tested rather than assumed. See §8.

## 7. Where this meets the wage ceiling [P]

*Alcohol Treatment Quarterly* 2016, PMID 27594760. 136 women in Oxford Houses:

> "There was a positive relationship between length of stay and wages. **Criminal
> history modified the association**... **length of stay had a significantly greater
> impact on wages for women with criminal convictions.**"

Against the barrier documented in H-09 — NCSL, `exhibits/barriers/`:

> the National Inventory "catalogs over **15,000 provisions of law**... that limit
> occupational licensing opportunities" and "over **6,000 mandatory** occupational
> licensing consequences"

> "**Licensed workers now comprise nearly 25 percent of all employed Americans.**"

**[I] The group the state has legally barred from a quarter of the labor market
benefited most from a housing model that costs the state nothing.** Cross-sectional,
length of stay again not randomized, and not proof. But if it holds it is the most
policy-relevant number in this file.

## 8. What would kill this, stated so it can happen

- **Income predicting recovery without predicting network growth.** That breaks the
  mediation and returns a plain economic model.
- **Outcomes tracking income amount, with source making no difference** once amount
  is controlled.
- **Oxford House's effect being explained by housing stability alone**, with
  self-support contributing nothing.
- **Any study isolating self-support from community and finding self-support
  inert.**

**The discriminating experiment:** hold total income and housing constant; vary
whether the income is earned or granted; measure participation and network as
mediators, using the Important Persons and Activities instrument and the PhenX
recovery-identity and recovery-capital protocols released in 2025.

**Nothing about that design is exotic. Every instrument in it already exists and is
validated.**

## 9. What this finding is, and is not

**It is not a finding that the model is true.** It is a finding that four
independent lines of evidence — a matched null on certificates, two randomized
residential trials with opposite reward structures and opposite results, a
systematic review in which self-run beat staff-run, and a randomized two-year
activity study showing the predicted shift — all point the same way, and that no
result in the file contradicts the model once mediation is allowed.

**A model that fits everything is also the model most at risk of being
unfalsifiable.** §8 exists so that this one can fail.

**So it remains a well-supported story.**

**And it will remain a well-supported story until someone cares about these people
enough to actually try to help them.** The evidence base for the most widely
available recovery support infrastructure in the United States is **five studies**
(PMID 40115346). The trials that would settle this have not been run — not because
they are difficult, expensive or ethically fraught, but because **a field that has
produced hundreds of studies confirming that people in prison have high rates of
addiction has produced almost none testing whether giving them a life reduces it.**

That is not a gap in the literature. **It is a gap in who was thought worth
studying**, and it is the same gap this entire case documents on the enforcement
side: the molecule is scheduled within months, and the question of what happens to
the person has been under-researched for thirty years.
