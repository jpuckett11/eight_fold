# CASE EIGHTFOLD / REMEDIATION
## What is owed to 42.4 million people, what it costs, and the one provision that would attach it to every action taken against them

**Opened 2026-08-31. Version 1: the frame and the established figures. Sections
marked TO BUILD are named deliberately rather than left implied.**

SEVENFOLD's `REMEDIATION.md` argues this for one molecule and one bill. **This
document generalises it, because the omission generalises.**

---

## 1. The scale, from the government's own survey [P]

SAMHSA, 2024 NSDUH. Exhibit `exhibits/nsduh/`, verified from the primary.

| | |
|---|---|
| Had a substance use disorder, 2024 | **48.4 million** (16.8% of people aged 12+) |
| Needed treatment | 52.6 million (18.2%) |
| Received it | **5.9 million (12.3%)** |
| **Did not** | **42.4 million (87.7%)** |
| Of those with **severe** SUD, received nothing | **72.1%** |
| Prevalence change 2021 → 2024 | **none** |

**Two of those lines carry the argument.**

**72.1%.** Say the severe figure, not the headline. Most of the 48.4 million is
classified mild, and an opponent will say so within a minute. Roughly **7.5 million
people meeting the criteria for a severe substance use disorder received no
treatment of any kind**, and that number is immune to the objection.

**No change since 2021.** Through the entire fentanyl response, every scheduling
action, every enforcement dollar. SEVENFOLD Finding 51 found the same across four
birth cohorts back to 1900: the relationship does not respond to anything done at
the supply end.

## 2. Why this is not a treatment-access essay

**The untreated population is what makes the substitution engine work.** SEVENFOLD
Finding 55 documents the cascade — prescription opioids, heroin, fentanyl,
nitazenes — and EIGHTFOLD Finding 01 shows each step moved people from the
low-severity access pathway to the high-severity one, at 6.4x for opioids and 9.5x
for stimulants.

**[I] Every turn of that cycle needs a buyer, and 42.4 million people with nowhere
to go are what guarantees one.** That claim is entered as **H-03** in
`HYPOTHESES.md` and is **the least tested proposition in this case**. It is stated
here because it is the organising idea, and flagged because it is the one most
likely to be believed without evidence, including by us.

## 2a. WHY those 42.4 million got nothing, which changes what remediation must do

**The claim itself is correct and stays.** 42.4 million people **got nothing** —
that is NSDUH's own phrasing and this case has never said more than it. OWG's
existing script already carries the three caveats a fair reader would demand: most
of the 48.4 million is classified mild, the severe figure is around 10.3 million,
and it is every substance rather than one drug. **That discipline is already in
place and this section does not correct it.**

What this section adds is the **mechanism**, and it materially changes what a
remediation program has to be built to do. [P, NSDUH 2024, pp. 50-51]

> "Among the **40.7 million adults** aged 18 or older in 2024 who had an SUD in the
> past year and did not receive substance use treatment, **95.6 percent (or 38.1
> million people) did not perceive that they needed treatment.** That is, they did
> not seek treatment and did not think they should get it."

Only **4.4% (1.8 million)** either sought treatment or thought they should. Of
those, **276,000 actually sought it.**

**[I] So for most of that population the binding constraint is not a closed door.
It is that no door was ever identified as relevant to them.** A remediation
argument built only on capacity — beds, slots, clinicians — answers the 4.4% and
misses the 95.6%.

**And a caution about reading the 95.6% too quickly in the other direction.** "Did
not perceive a need" is not the same as "did not need." Failure to recognize a
problem is a recognized feature of the condition itself, and it is also shaped by
whether help is visible, affordable and safe to seek. **Nothing here supports
concluding that 38.1 million people were fine.**

### The reasons data, and what it makes actionable

Among the 1.8 million adults who **did** perceive an unmet need, the stated reasons
[P, Table A.56B, multiple responses permitted]:

| Reason | % |
|---|---|
| Thought they should be able to handle it on their own | **75.5** |
| Not ready to start treatment | **65.0** |
| Not ready to stop or cut back | **59.5** |
| Treatment would cost too much | **45.3** |
| **Worried what people would think or say if they got treatment** | **43.2** |
| Not enough time | 41.3 |
| **Did not know how or where to get treatment** | **38.9** |
| Could not find a program or professional they wanted | 35.8 |

**[I] Three of these are policy variables, and one of them is manufactured by the
other half of this case.**

**Stigma — 43.2%.** Criminalisation raises the cost of being identified as a
person who uses drugs. **That is a direct, testable causal link between the
enforcement side of this case and the treatment side**, and it is the sharpest
thing in this section. Entered as **H-07** in `HYPOTHESES.md`.

**Not knowing where to go — 38.9%.** An information failure. North Dakota's entire
kratom appropriation is **$20,000 for a public awareness website** (SEVENFOLD
Finding 54) — which is the right *category* of intervention at an amount that
cannot work.

**Cost — 45.3%.** A straightforward access barrier for people who already want help.

**And the 95.6% remain the real frontier.** Reaching them is outreach,
identification and low-threshold contact, not beds. The costed package in §3 is
what someone needs *once they arrive*; it does not address why most never do.

**This section is why §5's provision is written as treatment AND measurement.** A
funding line that builds capacity nobody walks into fails on these numbers. One
that also reduces stigma, publishes where to go, and removes cost has a mechanism
against the three barriers that are actually measured.

**Note on how this section came to be written.** It was produced by Aegis while
checking a gap this document had flagged as unread. An earlier draft framed it as
correcting a claim that "42.4 million were denied treatment." **No such claim
appears anywhere in this case or in OWG's drafts** — the wording used throughout
has been "got nothing," which is accurate. The framing was Aegis's invention and is
recorded here because a correction aimed at a claim nobody made is itself a defect,
and because this file is supposed to show its own errors.

## 3. What it costs to pull one person all the way up [P, SEVENFOLD Finding 52]

Itemised, at full cost, per person per year:

| Component | Cost |
|---|---|
| Medication for opioid use disorder, methadone, all-in with counselling | **$6,552** (NIDA) |
| Contingency management at an effective dose | $750 |
| Recovery housing, six months | $10,500 |
| Vocational training with a certification | $4,000 |
| **Total** | **$21,802** |

**This is the number the argument turns on**, because it converts every
appropriation into a count of people. SEVENFOLD Finding 50: every dollar any state
has ever appropriated in connection with kratom is **$1,925,400** — **88 people**.

**TO BUILD:** the same arithmetic against total national drug-control spending
rather than one substance. The ONDCP National Drug Control Budget is the
instrument. **Not yet pulled.**

### The vocational line is the weakest component and should not be inherited unexamined

**Raised by Jay Puckett 2026-08-31, entered as H-08.** *"A cert is not a job that is
paying right away, or even paying enough for them to live comfortably."*

SEVENFOLD already establishes the negative: prison vocational education, propensity
score matched, showed **"no differences in any outcome"** between certificate holders
and controls (*IJOTCC* 2023). And it already states that a certificate is not an
occupation.

**What H-08 adds is the mechanism, which is in the case's own cited model and was
never applied.** Reinforcer pathology is defined by **"steep delay discounting,
overvaluation of substance reinforcement, and low reinforcement from substance-free
activities."** The remediation argument uses the third clause. **It never uses the
first.** A reward that arrives months later and then arrives small is the reward
shape that steep delay discounting predicts will fail.

**And the package contradicts itself on cost:**

| Component | Reward timing | Cost | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contingency management | **immediate** | **$750** | dose-response established |
| Vocational certification | **deferred, then modest** | **$4,000** | no measured effect after matching |

**5.3x more money on the component the evidence does not support.**

**[I] Provisional implication, pending H-08's economic test:** the $4,000 line
should be restructured toward **paid** training and income support during
transition, with placement judged on wage adequacy rather than completion. **Not yet
evidenced — time-to-first-income and wage-adequacy data have not been pulled**, and
the package is not being changed on intuition alone.

## 4. Upstream, where the ratio is best and the funding is worst

| | |
|---|---|
| Nurse-Family Partnership, full course, pregnancy to age two | **$10,000** |
| CDC lifetime cost, one nonfatal child maltreatment case | **$830,928** |
| **Ratio** | **83 : 1** |

Not a model. One published cost divided by another. **The program does not have
to work often. It has to work once in eighty-three times.**

And SEVENFOLD Finding 51: the attributable risk fraction for addiction to illicit
drugs from adverse childhood experiences is **64%**.

**What is actually funded [P, L-05, new 2026-08-31]:** federal home visiting
through MIECHV totals **$480,065,168** across 56 jurisdictions — **9.8%** of the
$4.9B that would cover every first-time Medicaid-enrolled mother for one cohort,
and **0.081%** of the CDC's $592B annual burden.

**Caveat that travels with this:** state appropriations are not counted. The
national total is a **floor**. Anyone saying "prevention is underfunded" without the
state numbers is overclaiming, and that work is L-05 step 2.

## 5. The provision, and why it generalises

SEVENFOLD asked for one line on one kind of bill. **The general form:**

> **Any legislative or regulatory action that schedules, restricts or criminalises
> a substance shall be accompanied by (a) an appropriation for treatment of the
> population affected, and (b) funding for the measurement required to know what
> the action did.**

**Why (a).** Utah proved the mechanism works: **$1,905,400** attached to a kratom
law, ongoing, in the budget bill — of which **$915,000** went to Adult Probation
and Parole, $684,000 to Prison Operations, $286,800 to District Courts, and **$0 to
treatment** (Finding 48). The vehicle exists. It has only ever been pointed one way.

**Why (b).** SEVENFOLD Finding 56: nobody can state how many people 7-OH has
killed, because no instrument was built. Tennessee is the only state that mandated
testing and **it funded none of it**. An action whose effect cannot be measured
cannot be evaluated, corrected, or defended.

**[I] This is not an argument against scheduling.** Findings 55 and 01 argue that
scheduling *without* these two provisions reproduces a documented failure at known
cost. The claim is about what must accompany the action, not whether to take it.

## 6. What this document still owes

Named rather than implied, so the gaps are visible:

- **TO BUILD — the national ledger.** Enforcement against treatment, per state.
  L-01 records why federal grant data cannot answer this: Byrne JAG is a rounding
  error against state corrections budgets, and Medicaid, the largest SUD payer, is
  not a grant. **It has to be built from state budget documents.** Utah and
  Tennessee are already in hand.
- **TO BUILD — the ONDCP budget arithmetic.** §3.
- **TO BUILD — treatment capacity against need, per state.** L-09 step 2.
- **TO BUILD — the reasons-for-not-receiving-treatment data.** The 2024 NSDUH
  carries a section on it. **It is the closest thing to an answer in the
  government's own words and it has not been read.**
- **TO TEST — H-03.** Whether treatment capacity predicts substitution uptake. The
  center of the argument and currently unevidenced.

## 7. The principle

SEVENFOLD's remediation closes on a sentence worth carrying forward unchanged: the
system is not evil and there is no conspiracy in it. Fifty states passed laws about
a molecule and not one budgeted a dollar for a person. **They were not hiding it.
Nobody asked.**

**EIGHTFOLD's version is the same sentence with the scope removed.** It is not one
molecule and it is not one year. It is every substance, every cycle, and the same
42.4 million people at the end of each one — who are not a side effect of the
market but the reason it always finds a buyer.
