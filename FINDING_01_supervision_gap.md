# EIGHTFOLD / FINDING 01
## The same drug produces severe addiction six to nine times more often when it is obtained outside a clinical structure. Every enforcement action in Finding 55's cascade moved people from the low side to the high side.

**Status:** CONFIRMED [P] from SAMHSA's 2024 National Survey on Drug Use and Health.
Causal interpretation is **[I]** and the competing explanation is stated in full at
§6. **Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-31.
**Opened on an observation by Jay Puckett.**

Exhibit: `exhibits/nsduh/2024_nsduh_annual_national_report.pdf`, hashed.

---

## 1. The observation being tested

Jay Puckett: *"any type of substance abuse disorder becomes severe when things
become so available."*

**The data supports it, and refines it into something more precise and more
useful.**

## 2. What NSDUH reports [P]

The survey classifies substance use disorder severity by DSM-5 criteria count, and —
critically — **it splits two drug classes by how the substance was obtained.**

| Population | Severe | Mild |
|---|---|---|
| **Opioid use disorder**, from **misuse** (heroin, or misusing prescription opioids) | **37.1%** | 42.4% |
| **Opioid use disorder**, from **use but not misuse** of prescribed opioids | **5.8%** | 82.8% |
| **CNS stimulant use disorder**, from **misuse** (cocaine, methamphetamine, misused prescription stimulants) | **48.3%** | 28.5% |
| **CNS stimulant use disorder**, from **use but not misuse** of prescribed stimulants | **5.1%** | 83.0% |

**Same chemistry. A 6.4-fold difference in severe-disorder rate for opioids and a
9.5-fold difference for stimulants**, separating on nothing but whether access ran
through a clinical structure.

Overall figures for context, same source: opioid use disorder **4.8 million** people
(19.3% severe); CNS stimulant use disorder **4.3 million** (37.8% severe).

## 3. The comparison that corrects the wording [P]

**Alcohol is the most available intoxicant in the country and it is not the most
severe.** Among 27.9 million people with alcohol use disorder: **19.2% severe**,
59.4% mild. Lower than misused opioids, far below illicit stimulants.

Adolescent marijuana use disorder runs **33.9% severe**.

**[I] So the driver is not availability by itself.** Alcohol is universally
available *and* legally supplied, labeled, dose-known and age-gated. What separates
the high-severity populations is not how easy a substance is to get. **It is whether
what you got was known.**

**The precise claim this finding supports: availability without supervision.**

## 4. Why this is the center of the case [I]

**The unsupervised pathway is exactly what the grey market is.** A gas-station
tablet with no verified dose, no labeled contents, no prescriber and no
pharmacist is the "misuse" column by construction. SEVENFOLD Finding 08 documents
a recalled 7-OH product where FDA's stated reason was that the tablet contained
**more of the active ingredient than the label said**, across roughly three million
tablets.

**And Finding 55's cascade is a machine for moving people from the low-severity
column to the high one.** Prescription opioids controlled in 2010 → heroin, deaths
**3,036 → 15,469** → interdiction → fentanyl → nitazenes. **Every step moved the
population further from any structure that knew what it was giving them.**

**Each turn of that cycle is therefore not severity-neutral.** On these numbers the
destination pathway carries six to nine times the severe-disorder rate of the one
people were pushed off.

## 5. What it costs, joined to the other half of the case

SEVENFOLD Finding 56 establishes that nobody can count the deaths, because no
instrument was built. This finding establishes that the population being created is
the severe one. And the treatment record, same NSDUH:

| | |
|---|---|
| Had an SUD, 2024 | **48.4 million** |
| Received treatment | 5.9 million (12.3%) |
| **Did not** | **42.4 million (87.7%)** |
| **Of those with SEVERE SUD, received nothing** | **72.1%** |
| Prevalence change, 2021-2024 | **none** |

**[I] The cascade manufactures severe disorder, and 72% of the people it
manufactures receive nothing at all.**

## 6. The competing explanation, stated in full because it is serious

**This is cross-sectional data and causation plausibly runs both ways.** A person
whose disorder is already severe is more likely to obtain opioids outside a
prescription — the severity may be producing the access mode rather than the
reverse. **Selection explains an unknown share of the 6.4x and 9.5x, and this
finding does not claim to have measured it.**

**What survives that objection, and it is enough:**

1. **The two populations differ enormously in severity.** That is a measurement, not
   an inference.
2. **Policy that moves people between those pathways is therefore not
   severity-neutral**, whichever direction the causal arrow points. A regime that
   pushes people off supervised supply is acting on a population whose severity
   profile differs by a factor of six to nine, and it has never measured what it did.
3. **Nobody has attempted the longitudinal study that would settle it.** That
   absence is itself reportable.

**Do not state 6.4x or 9.5x as a causal effect. State it as the difference between
two populations, with the selection caveat attached.** It is strong enough that way
and indefensible any other way.

## 7. What we are obliged to do with this

**This finding creates duties, and they are the reason to hold the standard rather
than the reward for holding it.**

**a. Say it where it can act, not only where it will be heard.** The HHS-OASH docket
closes **2026-09-10**. A comment saying the Department has never characterized the
severity consequences of moving a population off supervised supply is narrow,
answerable, and on the record permanently. A video is not.

**b. Ask the question nobody has funded.** The longitudinal study in §6 does not
exist. Saying "this needs studying" is weak; **naming the study, its design and the
agency that should fund it is not.**

**c. Carry the caveat every single time.** The selection problem is the first thing
a hostile reviewer will raise. Stating it ourselves is what makes the rest citable.
**A number that is 90% right and stated as 100% right is worth less than nothing to
the people it is about**, because it hands the other side a reason to discard the
whole file.

**d. Do not let this become an argument for prohibition or for permission.** These
numbers are not an argument that drugs should be freely available, and they are not
an argument that enforcement is always wrong. **They are an argument that a
government which moves populations between supply pathways owes them a measurement
of what that did.** That is the claim. Anything wider is not supported here.

**e. The 42.4 million are the point.** Every line in this finding is about supply.
The reason supply always finds a buyer is that **72% of the people with the most
severe form of this condition are given nothing.** No finding in this case is
finished until it connects back to that.

## 8. What this finding does NOT establish

- **No causal effect of access mode on severity.** §6.
- **No claim about prevalence.** NSDUH reports prevalence unchanged 2021-2024;
  nothing here contradicts that. The claim is about **severity distribution**, not
  how many people have a disorder.
- **No trend in treatment receipt.** The report states treatment questions were
  revised for 2022 and 2024, so **"4 years of data are not available to assess
  trends in the receipt of substance use treatment."** Prevalence may be trended.
  Receipt may not.
- **"Misuse" is NSDUH's category, not ours**, and it carries the survey's
  definitions. It is not a moral term and must not be used as one.
- **Most of the 48.4 million is classified mild.** Say so before anyone else does.
  The severe figure is the one that survives the objection.
