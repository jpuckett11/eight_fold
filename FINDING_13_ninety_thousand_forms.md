## 2. The number DOJ's press release rounded down [P]

The press release said Walmart's pharmacists reported pill mills "including through
thousands of 'refusal-to-fill' forms."

**The complaint, ¶155:**

> "The compliance team received **more than 90,000 refusal-to-fill forms from January
> 2012 through July 2015**, and continued to receive thousands more forms thereafter."

**Ninety thousand, in forty-three months.** About **seventy a day, every day, for
three and a half years.**

And what was in them, same paragraph:

> "members of the compliance team were made aware of both the identity and the
> egregious practices of certain pill-mill prescribers—including prescribers who wrote
> prescriptions for drugs based on **patients' drug-seeking requests rather than
> medical needs**, prescribers who were writing prescriptions for the same drugs and
> dosages for large numbers of patients, prescriptions for **cocktails of drugs known
> to be abused**, and prescriptions for excessive quantities"

## 3. The pharmacists were not permitted to stop it [P]

**This is the fact that changes the story, and it is not in the press release.**

Complaint, heading above ¶105 — Walmart's own Pharmacy Operations Manual policy
**POM 1311**:

> "instructed pharmacists to evaluate the patient-practitioner relationship and
> document refusals to fill, **but it prohibited pharmacists from blanket refusing to
> fill for a prescriber.**"

**A Walmart pharmacist who had identified a pill mill was required to refuse its
prescriptions one at a time, forever, and was forbidden to simply stop serving it.**
The complaint states the ban was not lifted until **2015**.

**[I] That is why there are ninety thousand forms.** The volume is not evidence the
system was working. **It is the arithmetic of a policy that made every pharmacist
re-fight the same prescriber every day and sent the paperwork to a team that was not
reading it.**

## 4. And the man responsible said what he thought [P]

Complaint ¶174. A field supervisor asked in **February 2015** whether the compliance
team "pull[ed] out any insights" from the refusal-to-fill information. The individual
"entrusted with the responsibility for monitoring the refusal-to-fill forms," B.N.,
answered:

> "The MOA that requires the reporting of the Refusal to fills expires in 30 days.
> **We have not invested a great amount of effort in doing analysis on the data since
> the agreement is virtually over.** Driving sales and patient awareness is a far
> better use of our Market Directors and Market manager's time."

**"Since the agreement is virtually over."** The reporting existed because a
memorandum of agreement required it, and the effort was being wound down because the
requirement was about to lapse. **The forms were compliance paperwork, and when the
obligation neared its end the analysis stopped.**

Then ¶175, which is the sentence Puckett's reading rests on:

> "B.N. also revealed his views of the opioid crisis in **September 2014**, when
> Walmart was asked to stock **overdose kits**. He noted that the items for the kits
> were '**terribly expensive and non-returnable, so use caution when deciding to stock
> these items.**' He forwarded the email thread to C.R. with the comment, '**More
> BS.**'"

**Naloxone. Non-returnable inventory. "More BS."**

**[I] Puckett's phrase was "no one cares or believes in them." ¶175 is that
sentiment, in writing, from the person whose job was the safety data.**

### 4a. The pharmacists were not the problem, and the complaint says so [P]

- ¶141: the **Weekly Key Metrics** report measured prescription counts, on-time fills
  and profits. "**The only metric associated with patient safety was a measurement of
  how accurately prescriptions were input.**"
- ¶142: incentive plans used **the number of prescriptions filled** as a factor in
  employee bonuses.
- ¶143: on Walmart's own AHRQ staffing surveys, employees reporting sufficient staff
  fell from **59% in June 2012 to 43% in October 2014.**
- ¶140: a manager told pharmacists to "hustle to the customer, hustle from station to
  station" because filling prescriptions "**is a battle of seconds.**"
- ¶539: a South Carolina pharmacist said validating out-of-state prescriptions would
  "negatively impact customer service, wait times, and hence directly our business
  metrics." A Missouri pharmacy manager said **there was nothing he could do about a
  problem prescriber** because store volume was "already down" and acting would
  interfere with "**sales goals.**"

**Ninety thousand forms is ninety thousand times a pharmacist did the right thing
under a policy designed to make it pointless.**

## 5. "Most were failed in their childhood" — tested, and it holds [P]

Puckett's second claim is the one this file would normally push back on. **It
survives.**

**The original ACE Study**, Felitti et al., 13,494 adults surveyed, 9,508 responding,
reprinted *Am J Prev Med* 2019, PMID 31104722:

> "Persons who had experienced **four or more categories of childhood exposure**,
> compared to those who had experienced none, had **4- to 12-fold increased health
> risks for alcoholism, drug abuse, depression, and suicide attempt**"

> "We found a **strong graded relationship** between the breadth of exposure to abuse
> or household dysfunction during childhood and multiple risk factors for several of
> the leading causes of death in adults."

**Graded means dose-response: more adversity, more risk, monotonically.** Replicated
since — *J Am Coll Health* 2023, n=1,402, found ≥4 ACEs carried **2.93x** the risk of
opioid misuse against zero ACEs, with a dose-response across the range. A community
mental health clinic sample, n=856, found **40% reported four or more.**

**And the scale [P]**, *JAMA Network Open* 2023, PMID 38055277, BRFSS 2019-2020,
820,673 adults representing 255 million:

> "An estimated **160 million of the total 255 million US adult population (63%) had 1
> or more ACE**"

> "**Twenty-two percent of adults had 4 or more ACEs and comprised 58% of the total
> economic burden**"

**Sixty-three percent of American adults carry at least one. The 22% carrying four or
more account for 58% of the cost.**

**Limits, stated:** the ACE Study is **cross-sectional and retrospective**, and adults
in poor health recalling their childhoods is a known bias that can inflate the
association. **The dose-response gradient is harder to explain away than the
association itself, and the finding rests on the gradient.** None of this establishes
that adversity *causes* addiction in any individual, and this file does not say it
does.

**[I] What it does establish is Puckett's premise: the population this case is about
is disproportionately a population that was harmed before it could consent to
anything.** They did not choose the household they were born into, and the graded
relationship between that household and what happened to them later is one of the
most replicated findings in population health.

## 6. Why the two halves belong in one finding

**[I] The complaint describes an organization that received ninety thousand written
warnings and decided reading them was worse use of staff time than selling. The ACE
literature describes a population that arrived at those pharmacy counters already
carrying a fourfold to twelvefold elevated risk, through nothing they did.**

**Neither half is about a failure of individual character, and both are usually
narrated as if they were.**

**And note where the accountability landed.** The company paid **$50 million** with
**no determination of liability**, thirteen years after the conduct began. Finding 12
computed that as **two and a half hours** of federal improper payments. **The people
on the other end of those ninety thousand forms were, over the same period, subject to
arrest, license revocation and the 15,000 statutory barriers in Finding 06.**

## 7. What this finding does NOT claim

- **These are allegations.** DOJ's own release: "allegations only; there has been no
  determination of liability." The stipulated dismissal resolved the case **without a
  finding**. Every quotation in §2-4 is the United States' pleading, not a verdict.
- **B.N. and C.R. are initials used by the complaint.** No individual is named here
  and none should be.
- **The complaint is one side's document.** Walmart's answer and defenses are on the
  same docket and have **not** been pulled. **That is action item 1.**
- **The ACE evidence is associational**, retrospective and subject to recall bias.
  **No causal claim is made about any individual.**
- **Nothing here says pharmacists were blameless.** DOJ alleges pharmacists also
  filled prescriptions they knew were invalid. §4a is about the conditions, not an
  exoneration.
