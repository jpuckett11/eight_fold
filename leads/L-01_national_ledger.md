# L-01 — The ledger

**Status: ATTEMPTED 2026-08-31. Federal grant data CANNOT answer this. Redirected
to state budgets.** Not citable.

## The question

Per state: dollars to enforcement against dollars to treatment.

SEVENFOLD proved it for one substance in two states. Finding 48: Utah appropriated
**$1,905,400** in connection with its kratom law — probation, prisons, courts,
parole board, crime labs — **and $0 to treatment**, while cutting $120,600 from the
agency that runs the product registry. Finding 49: Tennessee appropriated **$0** and
handed county medical examiners an unfunded testing mandate. Finding 50: every
dollar any state has ever appropriated in connection with kratom is **$1,925,400**,
of which **$0** went to treatment.

**L-01 asks whether that shape holds across all substances.**

## What was attempted, and why it failed

Pulled two federal block grants from USAspending as a proxy ratio:

- **93.959** SAMHSA Substance Use Prevention, Treatment and Recovery Services Block
  Grant — 623 award records, 143 recipients
- **16.738** Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant — 2,600 award records,
  **966 distinct recipients**

**The comparison is invalid in three separate ways and would have produced a
backwards answer:**

1. **Aggregation.** SABG goes to roughly one state agency per state, so a
   latest-award-per-state method works. **JAG is distributed to hundreds of local
   police departments, sheriffs and counties** — California alone returns 253
   records. The same method captures one arbitrary local grantee and undercounts by
   orders of magnitude.
2. **Truncation.** The JAG pull hit a 26-page cap and is incomplete. Recent award
   years are also thin in the returned set.
3. **The category error, which is fatal even if 1 and 2 were fixed.** Byrne JAG is a
   few hundred million dollars nationally. **Actual enforcement spending is state
   and local corrections and policing — tens of billions — and none of it appears in
   federal award data.** Meanwhile the largest payer for SUD treatment is
   **Medicaid**, which is not a grant and is likewise absent.

**[I] A federal-grants ratio would have shown treatment exceeding enforcement by
more than twenty to one. That answer is an artifact of looking in the one place
where enforcement money is not.**

## What would actually answer it

**State budget documents, the way SEVENFOLD built Utah.** Finding 48 was constructed
by reading line items in Utah HB 3 tagged to a specific bill. That is the method and
it does not generalize cheaply — it is fifty budget documents, read.

Start with the two states already held in SEVENFOLD's exhibits: **Utah** and
**Tennessee**. Then the states with the largest SUD populations, using L-09's
per-state figures to prioritize.

Secondary sources worth checking before doing it by hand: NASBO State Expenditure
Report, BJS Justice Expenditure and Employment Extracts, and state Medicaid SUD
spend via CMS. **All are [S] until reconciled against a budget document.**

## Controls

- Any per-state figure must name its **fiscal year and its source document**.
- Enforcement and treatment are usually in **different appropriations bills**, so a
  single-bill search will produce a false zero. Finding 48 found Utah's enforcement
  money in the omnibus budget bill, not in the kratom bill itself.
