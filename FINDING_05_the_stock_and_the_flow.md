# EIGHTFOLD / FINDING 05
## The national survey counts how many people have a substance use disorder. It does not count how many got one. And 95.6% of the people it counts do not think they need help.

**Status:** CONFIRMED [P], including a controlled negative.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-31.
**Opened at Jay Puckett's objection to age-banded analysis:**

> *"a mortgage borrower at 18-29 has a bad divorce at 30 and spirals. Breaking this
> into an age range does and proves nothing, because addiction can start at any
> point. A large number are prone and do not even know or realize."*

**Both halves of that check out. The second one checks out at 95.6%.**

Exhibits in `exhibits/nsduh/`.

---

## 1. The methodological half, and it applies to this case file first

Every age-banded number in EIGHTFOLD — Finding 03's detailed brackets, H-11's
cohorts, Finding 04's delinquency-by-age — is a **cross-section**. It photographs a
population at one moment. **The 18-29 band in 2021 and the 18-29 band in 2026 are not
the same people.**

**Puckett's example is precisely the case the design cannot see.** Someone who takes
a mortgage at 28, divorces at 30 and spirals at 31 appears in the 18-29 band as a
current borrower, and in the 30-39 band as a delinquent one. **Nothing in a
cross-section connects those two observations.** The event, the sequence and the
person are all invisible. What the data shows is that one box got worse and another
box got better, with no way to know whether anybody moved between them.

**That is not a small caveat. It is the reason section 9d of Finding 04 had to be
withdrawn**, and it is a stronger statement of the ecological-fallacy warning already
standing in H-11.

**Recorded as a standing rule in `METHOD.md`:** age-banded cross-sections describe
populations, never trajectories. **Any EIGHTFOLD claim about what happens to a person
over time requires panel data following the same people, and must say so or not be
made.**

## 2. The instrument problem underneath it [P, with control]

**NSDUH measures prevalence — the stock. It does not measure onset of disorder — the
flow.**

**Controlled, because a negative without a control is worthless.** The 2024 annual
national report:

| Search | Hits |
|---|---|
| `initiat*` (control, known present) | **68** |
| Tables on initiation of **use** | A.21A, A.22B, A.23B; Figures 31-34 |
| `onset` / `incidence` / `new cases` / `first developed` of **disorder** | **0** |

The single `incidence` match in the whole document is a bibliography entry for an
unrelated paper on suicide trends.

**So the survey asks in great detail at what age a person first drank, first smoked,
first used marijuana. It never asks at what age a person became addicted.**

**[I] That is the difference between counting how many people are in the water and
counting how many fell in.** A prevalence series can hold perfectly steady while
everyone in it is replaced. It cannot tell you whether the flow of new cases is
rising, whether it is shifting later in life, or what event preceded it — which is
exactly what Puckett's divorce example asks, and exactly what a country would need in
order to intervene before rather than after.

**Nothing here says the data cannot be collected.** Longitudinal instruments that
measure onset exist and are federally funded: **NESARC** followed 34,653 adults
across two waves; **Add Health** has followed one cohort from adolescence into their
forties; **NLSY97** runs the same design. **None was pulled for this finding and none
is relied on.** They are named because the remedy is not hypothetical — it is a
question of which instrument the annual headline is built from.

## 3. "A large number are prone and do not even know" — this is the number [P]

2024 NSDUH, Figures 72 and 73, Table A.55AB. **Among people who had a substance use
disorder in the past year and did not receive treatment:**

| | Did NOT perceive a need for treatment |
|---|---|
| **Adults 18+** | **95.6%** — 38.1 million of 40.7 million |
| **Adolescents 12-17** | **93.3%** — 1.5 million of 1.7 million |

**Of 40.7 million untreated adults with a diagnosable substance use disorder, 38.1
million did not seek treatment and did not think they should.** Fewer than one in
twenty perceived an unmet need. **276,000 — 0.7% — actually sought treatment and did
not get it.**

**Read that against the treatment-gap figure this case has been carrying.** The
familiar framing is that 87.6% of people with a substance use disorder get no
treatment, and the implied villain is access: cost, insurance, waitlists, distance.
**Access is a real barrier and it is not the main one at this stage.** For 38.1
million people the process never reaches the question of access, because it never
reaches the question of whether anything is wrong.

## 4. Which reframes the remedy, and this is the part that matters

**[I] A treatment appropriation reaches the 4.4%.** Every recommendation this case
file has produced so far — Finding 52 and Finding 56 in SEVENFOLD, the funding
arguments in `REMEDIATION.md` — is built to serve people who are looking for help.
**That population is 1.8 million adults. The population with the disorder is 40.7
million.**

**Building more doors does not help someone who does not know they are in a
building.**

**And it is the strongest available support for H-07** (`HYPOTHESES.md`), which holds
that criminalization manufactures the barrier. **A condition that carries arrest,
license loss and job loss is a condition people have every practical reason not to
recognize in themselves.** 95.6% is what non-recognition looks like at national
scale.

**Stated as an inference, not a finding.** NSDUH measures *perceived* need. It cannot
separate a person who genuinely does not meet their own threshold for concern from a
person who will not say so on a federal survey, and this file does not pretend to
know the split. **What is established is the number, and that the number is 95.6%.**

## 5. What this finding does not claim

- **It does not claim NSDUH is deficient overall.** It is the best population
  instrument the country has. **The claim is narrow: it measures a stock and the
  policy question is about a flow.**
- **It does not claim the 95.6% are wrong about themselves.** DSM-5 SUD spans mild
  to severe; a person meeting two criteria for a mild disorder may reasonably not
  consider themselves in need of treatment, and the pooled figure does not separate
  severity. **That caveat travels with the number.**
- **It does not resolve Puckett's divorce example.** Nothing here identifies what
  precipitates onset. That requires the panel data named in section 2, and this file
  does not have it.

## 7. Addendum, same day — the panel data, pulled at Puckett's instruction

> *"pull nesarc and add health"*

**Done, with one limit stated up front: these are PUBLISHED RESULTS, not microdata.**
NESARC and Add Health individual-level files both require a data use agreement and
were not obtained. **Nothing below is an original analysis of either panel.** Every
figure is attributed to its paper, and the abstracts are captured in
`exhibits/panel/`. **Section 6 item 2 is therefore partially complete, not
complete.**

### 7a. "Addiction can start at any point" — the distribution says the tail is long [P]

**Lancet Psychiatry 2023**, PMID 37531964. WHO World Mental Health surveys,
**156,331 respondents, 32 surveys, 29 countries**, age-of-onset distributions and
cumulative incidence to age 75 for 13 DSM-IV disorders:

> "Conditional probabilities of first onset peaked at approximately age 15 years,
> with a **median age of onset of 19 years (IQR 14-32)** for male respondents and
> **20 years (12-36)** for female respondents."

> "**By age 75 years, approximately half the population can expect to develop one or
> more of the 13 mental disorders considered**"

Morbid risk by 75: **46.4% of men, 53.1% of women.** Alcohol use disorder is the most
prevalent of the thirteen for men.

**Read the interquartile range, not just the median.** It runs to **32 for men and 36
for women**. **A quarter of all first onsets happen later than that** — which is the
right tail, and the right tail is where Puckett's divorce-at-30 sits.

**Stated precisely, because the temptation to overstate here is obvious:** these are
figures for **any of thirteen disorders**, not for substance use disorder alone. The
paper reports AUD among the most prevalent for men; it does not give this file a
SUD-specific onset curve. **The long tail is established for the composite. The
SUD-specific version is not, and is not claimed.**

### 7b. The onset really is a flow, and it has a national rate [P]

**Compr Psychiatry 2013**, PMID 23522830, NESARC Waves 1 and 2 — the same people
interviewed in 2001-2002 and again in 2004-2005:

> "the **3-year incidence rates** of DSM-IV substance use, mood and anxiety disorders
> ranged from **0.65% (bipolar II) to 5.2% (alcohol abuse)**"

**That is the number NSDUH cannot produce and this design can.** Same people, two
points in time, new cases counted. **The flow exists as a measurable quantity. It is
simply not what the annual national headline is built from.**

**Psychol Addict Behav 2015**, PMID 26727008, same waves, computes sex-specific
3-year incidence of alcohol and drug use disorders and its predictors — and closes
by saying the work still needs doing:

> "**Prospective research is needed to identify predictors of incident DSM-5 AUDs and
> DUDs and their underlying mechanisms**"

**Written in 2015 about a survey fielded in 2001-2005. It is 2026.**

### 7c. Add Health: life events beat genetics, in the direction Puckett named [P]

**Drug Alcohol Depend 2017**, PMID 28412301. **12,437 youth from the nationally
representative Add Health sample, followed from mid-adolescence through early
adulthood.** Title, verbatim: *"Environmental risks outweigh dopaminergic genetic
risks for alcohol use and abuse from adolescence through early adulthood."*

> "**Endogenous and exogenous stressful life events** and social norms supportive of
> alcohol use from parents and peers **were significant predictors** of alcohol use,
> intoxication, and alcohol use disorder, with consistent patterns across males and
> females. In contrast, a **dopamine-system genetic risk score was not associated**
> with alcohol use trajectories nor alcohol use disorder in early adulthood."

> "**No evidence of gene-environment interactions emerged.**"

**Stressful life events predicted the disorder. The genetic risk score did not.**
That is the mechanism in Puckett's example — the event, not the predisposition — and
it is measured on a national panel following the same 12,437 people forward.

**Limits carried:** it is a **dopaminergic** risk score, one pathway, not a modern
polygenic score, and absence of association with that score is not absence of
heritability. **The claim is what the paper claims: on this panel, environment
outweighed this genetic measure.**

### 7d. And it moves H-11 too

**Drug Alcohol Depend 2010**, PMID 20018459, NESARC, 4,782 adults with lifetime
alcohol dependence: the best-fit model splits age at onset into two subgroups with a
**cut-off at 22 years**, early onset carrying higher rates of nearly all other
addictive disorders.

**[I] Onset is bimodal, not a single curve peaking in the twenties.** An early group
and a later group, behaving differently. **Any age-band analysis averages the two
together and describes neither** — which is section 1's rule, arriving from the
clinical literature rather than from methodology.

### 7e. What is now closed, and what is still open

**Closed:** onset is measurable, has a national rate, has a long right tail, and is
predicted by life events rather than by the genetic measure tested. **Puckett's
objection was correct on every count that the published literature can speak to.**

**Still open, and it needs the microdata:** onset *specifically for substance use
disorder* by single year of age, and onset conditioned on a **named** preceding event
— divorce, job loss, injury, bereavement. **NESARC's two waves and Add Health's five
can both answer that. Neither can be answered from abstracts.** Data use agreements
are the next step, and they are applications, not research problems — the same shape
as the vintage-default and HCAI pulls in Finding 04.
