## 5. The federal service money, and a $4 billion figure thrown away [P]

USAspending, awarding subtier **SAMHSA**, grant awards FY2024-25:
**3,000 awards, $37.99 billion.** Of that, **State Opioid Response $9.85B** and
**block grants $16.85B**.

**The employment number required discarding a big one first.** Keyword-matching the
award descriptions for employment returned **430 awards and $4.016 billion**. **It is
noise.** USAspending's description field carries the entire project abstract, so a
$317 million Florida State Opioid Response block award matches because the word
appears somewhere inside it. **24 State Opioid Response awards alone carry $2.07B of
that $4B and not one of them is an employment program.**

**The defensible measure is the project title.** Awards actually *named* for
employment:

**24 awards. $73.8 million. Out of $37.99 billion. 0.19%.**

They are genuine: LifeWorks supported employment for recently housed transition-age
youth, the OYEAH Supported Employment Program, Pascua Yaqui Tribe Centered Spirit
Supportive Employment, the Kentucky Supported Employment Program, NARA NW.
**Real programs, at roughly $3.5 million each, and there are twenty-four of them in
the country.**

**[I] Three independent funding streams, three measurements, same answer.** NIH
research: recovery support loses to pharmacology by 7 to 17 to one (Finding 07).
Indiana settlement spending: employment 2.1%. SAMHSA service delivery: employment
0.19%. **Finding 02 said self-support is what the recovery evidence actually tracks.
No one is funding it at scale, through any of the three doors.**

## 6. Method note: the same trap, caught twice in one night

**Two convenient figures were discarded in this session** — NIH "occupational
licensing" at $44.8M (Finding 07 §7) and SAMHSA "employment" at $4.016B. **Both would
have strengthened arguments in this case file. Both were free-text keyword artifacts
and neither survived a title check.**

**The rule that caught them is now standing:** where a database's searchable text is
a full abstract, a keyword total measures what documents *mention*, not what money
*does*. **Report the title-restricted figure, or report nothing.**

## 8. What this finding does NOT claim

- **Indiana is one state and one year.** Nothing here generalizes to the other 49,
  and Indiana's own reporting format may differ from theirs in ways that change the
  arithmetic.
- **"Unspent" is not "misused."** Money can be lawfully held for a multi-year plan.
  The filing's own third category is "designated," and 46 units used it.
- **No allegation is made against any named unit, official or county.** Every figure
  is what the government itself filed.
- **The keyword categories in §4 overlap** and are not a partition. **Treat them as
  shares of attention, not a budget.**
- **0.19% is a share of SAMHSA grant awards, not of all federal addiction spending.**
  Medicaid is far larger and is not in this pull.
