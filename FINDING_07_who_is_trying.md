# EIGHTFOLD / FINDING 07
## Federal research money goes to the molecule over the life by something between 7 and 17 to one. The people running the employment experiments are the VA, and the database reports their budget as zero.

**Status:** CONFIRMED [P] from the federal grant database, with controls run and two
instrument defects found, reported and carried.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-31.
**Opened at Jay Puckett's question: "what about funding, who is trying anything."**

Exhibits and the re-runnable script in `exhibits/funding/`.

## 1. What was asked

**Finding 02** closed by saying the discriminating experiment on recovery "has never
been run," and that the evidence base for the most widely available recovery support
infrastructure in the country is **five studies**. That was an argument from the
literature. **This is the same claim tested against the money.**

## 2. Method, and what it can and cannot support

NIH RePORTER, the federal grant database, fiscal years 2024 and 2025. Text search
across project title, abstract and terms, requiring **all** words present.

**Controls first, before any low number was believed:** `opioid` returns **4,778**
projects, `addiction` returns **5,434**. **The search works.**

**What this cannot do:** an all-words text match finds projects that *mention* the
terms, not projects *about* them. So every figure below is reported two ways — the
raw match, and the subset whose **title** also names the topic. **The truth is
between them, and the ratio is the usable quantity, not the absolute.**

**Raw: 6.9 to 1. Title-confirmed: 17.4 to 1.**

**Both groups double-count internally** — one project can match several terms — so
each is an upper bound on its own side. **That inflates both numerators, which is
why the ratio survives it.**

**Two fiscal years. Two hundred and thirty-four unique on-topic recovery-support
projects at $63.5 million, against a pharmacology and medication line measured in
the billions.**

**[I] This is not an argument that medication research is wrong or overfunded.**
Buprenorphine and naloxone save lives and the evidence for them is strong. **The
finding is the ratio.** The country is funding the question *what molecule* at
between seven and seventeen times the rate it funds the question *what life*, and
Finding 02 established that the second question has better evidence behind it than
anyone has bothered to build on.

## 4. Recovery housing is the number that should be embarrassing [P]

**$3.8 million.** Ten projects across two fiscal years whose titles actually name
housing or residence.

**Set that against Finding 02:** recovery housing produced a randomized net benefit
of **~$29,000 per person, statistically significant**, while Job Corps' benefits came
in below its costs. **The intervention with a positive, randomized, published
cost-benefit result receives less federal research funding in two years than a single
mid-sized clinical trial costs.**

And `oxford house` as a search term returns **nine projects** in two fiscal years.

## 6. The VA finding, and the defect that nearly hid it [P]

**Of the seventeen employment-specific projects in this corpus, seven are VA** —
supported employment for veterans with opioid use disorder, therapeutic work activity
augmenting early substance treatment, customized competitive employment, supported
employment access for polytrauma and TBI.

**Every one of them reports an award amount of $0.**

**That is a database artifact, not a budget.** Checked directly: **253 VA projects in
this corpus, zero with a nonzero award amount. 2,098 NIDA projects, 2,098 with one.**
VA does not report intramural award amounts to RePORTER.

**[I] So the institution running the largest concentration of employment-based
recovery interventions in the federal government is invisible to any dollar-weighted
analysis of federal addiction research.** Anyone ranking this field by money — which
is what a budget office does — sees the VA contributing nothing.

**This bears directly on a hypothesis Puckett stated earlier the same day:** *"what
the military does can be done in the civ world; there are already existing facilities
like this."* **The federal government is already running the civilian version's
prototype. It is running it inside the VA, for veterans only, and reporting it at
zero.**

## 7. The defect that would have produced a false finding, recorded [P]

The search `occupational licensing` returned **38 projects and $44.8 million** — a
number that would have fit this case file beautifully beside Finding 06.

**It is 100% noise.** Zero of the 38 mention licensing in the title. The top
institutes are NIOSH, NHLBI and NICHD. *Occupational* matched occupational health;
*licensing* matched technology-transfer boilerplate in abstracts.

**The figure is discarded and is recorded here because it is the reason every other
number in this finding got a title check.** A convenient number that confirms the
thesis is exactly the one to distrust.

## 8. What this finding does NOT establish

- **It is not a complete picture of addiction funding.** NIH RePORTER excludes SAMHSA
  block grants, the State Opioid Response grants, and **opioid settlement money**,
  which is roughly $50 billion and is the largest live pot in the field. **None of
  it is in this pull.**
- **It does not measure quality or impact**, only dollars and counts.
- **The title-check subset is too strict** in the other direction — a project can be
  about employment without "employ" in its title. **That is why both bounds are
  published rather than the flattering one.**
- **It does not establish that shifting money would work.** That is H-08 and H-10,
  and Finding 02 §8 still specifies the experiment nobody has run.

## 9. Action

1. **Pull opioid settlement spending.** ~$50B, state by state, and the accountability
   record is the live story. **This is the biggest gap in the funding thread and it
   is the next pull.**
2. **Pull SAMHSA block grant and State Opioid Response allocations** from USAspending
   — the service-delivery money RePORTER cannot see.
3. **Ask the VA directly what it spends on supported employment for substance use
   disorder.** RePORTER will never answer it. A FOIA or a budget-document pull will.
4. **Carry §6 with Finding 02 and REMEDIATION.** The prototype for the civilian
   program this case keeps arguing for already exists, is federally run, and is
   restricted to people who served.
