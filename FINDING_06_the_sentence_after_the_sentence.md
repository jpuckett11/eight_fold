# EIGHTFOLD / FINDING 06
## The inventory of collateral consequences says, in its own words, that the bars frequently apply without regard to how much time has passed or what the person has done since.

**Status:** CONFIRMED [P] from the maintaining body's own published text.
**Analyst:** Aegis, for Obsidian Watch Group. **Date:** 2026-08-31.
**Opened at Jay Puckett's statement:**

> *"there is no reason a young adult who changes his life should pay for the rest of
> his life by being barred from a lic because of his past"*

**That is not a slogan. It is a description of how the system is documented to work,
and the documentation is maintained by a federally funded body that says so plainly.**

Exhibits in `exhibits/barriers/`. Registered against H-07 and H-09.

---

## 1. The claim, and the standard it has to meet

Puckett's statement contains two propositions:
1. **The bar persists regardless of rehabilitation.**
2. **The bar persists regardless of elapsed time.**

**Both are checkable, and neither has to be argued from anecdote.**

## 2. The National Inventory says both, unprompted [P]

The **National Inventory of Collateral Consequences of Conviction** is maintained by
the Council of State Governments Justice Center under Bureau of Justice Assistance
funding. Its own landing page, captured
`exhibits/barriers/niccc_landing_20260831.html`:

> "some collateral consequences apply **without regard to the relationship between
> the crime and opportunity being restricted**, such as the revocation of a business
> license after conviction of **any felony**. Frequently consequences also apply
> **without consideration of the time passed between the conviction and the
> opportunity being sought or the person's rehabilitation efforts since the
> conviction.**"

**That single sentence contains both of Puckett's propositions and it is the
inventory's own characterization of its own contents.** This finding does not have to
infer it, model it or estimate it.

**Note the middle clause too.** *Without regard to the relationship between the crime
and the opportunity.* A conviction for one thing bars a license for an unrelated
thing. **The bar is not a safety judgment about this person and this job. It is a
status.**

## 3. The scale [P]

From `exhibits/barriers/ncsl_barriers_to_work_criminal_records.pdf`, National
Conference of State Legislatures:

- The Inventory "catalogs over **15,000 provisions of law** in both statute and
  regulatory codes that limit occupational licensing opportunities"
- and "over **6,000 mandatory** occupational licensing consequences"
- "**Licensed workers now comprise nearly 25 percent of all employed Americans**"

**Independently corroborated on a separate page** from a different organization's
project, `exhibits/barriers/fcl_*.html`, CSG Justice Center Fair Chance Licensing:

> "**Nearly 1 in 4 jobs in the U.S. require a government-issued license.**"

**Two sources, two documents, same quarter of the labor market.** And 6,000 of the
provisions are **mandatory** — meaning no board discretion, no individualized review,
no place to present evidence of rehabilitation because the statute does not permit
the board to consider it.

**Honest limit, carried from the earlier capture and still unresolved:** a widely
circulated figure of "more than 40,000 total collateral consequences" could not be
verified. The NICCC inventory is a Drupal search interface that renders no totals
server-side and exposes no JSON endpoint to an unauthenticated fetch. **Use the NCSL
numbers, which are in a captured document. The 40,000 remains [S] and unverified.**

## 4. What it looks like on one person [S]

CSG Justice Center, Fair Chance Licensing, same capture:

> "Lester Young was sent to prison when he was **19**. When he got out **22 years
> later**, he had a dream of becoming a home inspector in South Carolina. But **after
> taking all the required classes, he discovered he would not be allowed to take the
> licensing exam** due to his felony conviction."

**Twenty-two years, and the coursework completed, and he could not sit the exam.**
**[S], and labeled as such** — it is an advocacy organization's account of one
person and OWG has not independently verified it. **It is included because it is the
shape of the thing, not because it is the evidence.** Section 2 is the evidence.

## 5. Why this belongs in an addiction case file and not only a reentry one

**[I] The connection is not sentiment. It is the mechanism this case keeps
documenting.**

- **Finding 02** established, across four independent evidence lines, that
  **self-support** — earning your own way, not receiving support — is what the
  recovery data actually tracks. Oxford House residents **pay their own rent**, and
  that model produced a **+$29,000 per person** net benefit while Job Corps'
  benefits came in below its costs.
- **Finding 02 §7** found the effect **strongest** for the group the state has
  legally barred: length of stay had a *significantly greater* impact on wages for
  women with criminal convictions.
- **Finding 05** established that **95.6%** of untreated adults with a substance use
  disorder do not perceive a need for treatment, and **H-07** holds that
  criminalization manufactures that non-recognition.

**Put those together.** The mechanism that the evidence says works is earning your
own way. **Fifteen thousand provisions of law stand between that mechanism and a
quarter of the labor market**, six thousand of them mandatory, applying — by the
inventory's own account — without regard to time elapsed or rehabilitation
performed.

**[I] The state is not neutral between recovery and relapse here. It funds treatment
on one side and, on the other, maintains the largest single obstacle to the thing the
treatment is supposed to produce.**

## 6. What this finding does NOT claim

- **It does not claim all licensing bars are unjustified.** The Inventory names
  legitimate ones itself: firearms after violent conviction, working with children
  after abuse, positions of trust after fraud. **The finding is about bars that apply
  without relation, without time limit and without individualized review**, which the
  Inventory distinguishes from those.
- **It does not quantify how many of the 15,000 are permanent.** The Inventory says
  "frequently." **It does not say what fraction, and neither does this file.**
  Producing that fraction requires querying the Inventory field by field, and the
  interface does not permit it programmatically.
- **It does not establish that removing the bars reduces substance use.** That is
  H-09, and it is untested. **The recidivism literature is adjacent but is not the
  same outcome.**
- **No causal claim is made from Lester Young.**

## 7. Action

1. **Query NICCC state by state for the duration field.** The claim that needs
   evidence is not "bars exist" — that is established — but **what share carry no
   time limit and no discretion.** That number would be the finding.
2. **Pull the Fair Chance Licensing state-by-state maps** and count states that have
   adopted (a) a direct-relationship requirement, (b) a lookback limit, and (c) a
   pre-application determination. **Reform is happening and this file should say
   where, not only where it has not.**
3. **Carry this with Finding 02 in any remediation document.** The evidence that
   self-support drives recovery and the evidence that self-support is legally
   obstructed are two halves of one argument, and separating them weakens both.
4. **Do not use Lester Young in an OWG public artifact without independent
   verification.** [S] stays [S].
